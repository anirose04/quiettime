# quiet time — updated garden edition

This is a static html, css, and javascript progressive web app. All interface labels are lowercase, the app background is sampled from your garden photo, and the homepage is one scrollable collection over the garden photo: activity cards (icon and name only) grouped into games, words, explore, and away from the screen, gently coloured by kind — moss green for puzzles and games, dusty mauve for writing, olive-brown for reading, slate blue for learning, earthy sage for timer activities — followed by a compact quiet-session control.

## included
- sudoku, minesweeper, and solitaire, playable inside the app
- **the psyc paper** (`#psyc`): a research-literature digest for metacognition, learning and judgment under uncertainty, in the history paper's newspaper style. Two sections that never mix their claims:
  - **the literature** — hand-curated, *verified digests* (`psyc-issues.js`), each centred on one 2020+ empirical article: 01 research question · 02 inside the study · 03 what they found · 04 why it matters · 05 a little question · 06 the literature connection · 07 keep this, with claim-level citations and APA references (`PSYC_BIB`). Issues so far: Jaeger & Fiorella (2024, cue use and instructional visuals), Onan et al. (2022, interpreting effort), Murphy, Hoover & Castel (2023, judgments of importance and study time), connected to Koriat (1997), Oppenheimer (2004) and Metcalfe & Finn (2008).
  - **fresh off the press** — a discovery feed (`data/psyc-feed.json`) refreshed weekly by `.github/workflows/psyc-feed.yml` running `scripts/psyc-feed.mjs`. Cards show bibliographic records only, filterable by 30/90/180 days, preprints, reviews and meta-analyses, and newly indexed works; sortable by relevance-weighted recency, newest, recently discovered, relevance, author or journal. Save, mark read, dismiss, open the original or an open-access copy, or add to the **review queue**.
  - an **archive** (digests, kept findings, saved papers; search; organise by topic, author, year, framework or method; bibliography download; JSON notes export/import) and a **review queue** (discovered → reviewed, with a digest-draft export). Status words are strict: *discovered* (record identified), *reviewed* (methods, results and discussion actually examined), *verified digest* (every substantive claim checked and cited). The feed never produces summaries and never marks anything reviewed or verified.
  - **watchlists** live in `data/psyc-watchlists.json` (topics, authors by OpenAlex ID, journals by ISSN, foundational papers, related DOIs) and can be edited on GitHub without touching code. A keyword alone never makes a paper relevant: it needs a title match, two separate topic matches, or a topic match plus an independent signal (a watched author or a citation of a foundational/collected paper), must be in psychology, social sciences or neuroscience, and must mention learning, memory, judgment, confidence or similar.
  - **providers and limits** — OpenAlex (search, `referenced_works` citation links, topics, open-access locations; free, but anonymous search is rate-limited — add a free `OPENALEX_API_KEY` repository secret for reliable weekly runs; 10 requests/s without a key, 30 with one); Crossref REST API (online, issue and deposit dates, preprint relations, correction notices; polite pool via a `CROSSREF_MAILTO` secret; list queries about 3/s polite, 1/s public); PubMed E-utilities (Meta-Analysis / Systematic Review / Review publication types where indexed; 3 requests/s, 10 with `NCBI_API_KEY`). No scraping, no Google Scholar. Abstracts are used only for relevance scoring and are never shown or stored.
  - **what is verified** — in the feed: that a record exists and identifies the work (DOI, title, authors, source, dates from OpenAlex/Crossref), whether it cites a foundational or collected paper (OpenAlex reference lists — citing is not agreeing), and article type (PubMed type, else Crossref/OpenAlex type, else title wording, always labelled). Not verified: anything about methods or findings. In digests: every substantive claim, against the full article.
  - **dates** — the card shows the original online date (Crossref `published-online`, else OpenAlex), notes when the journal issue came later (online first), and labels *newly indexed* works (added to OpenAlex in the last 30 days but published earlier) separately. If a refresh fails, the previous feed is kept and the app shows the last successful update and the failure.
- **the history paper** (`#history`): small, self-contained history issues to read in 3–7 minutes, typeset like a little typewritten newspaper. Each issue has a story, a verified excerpt from a period text (from Project Gutenberg), one or two questions with sourced explanations, a curious connection, and a sources list; every claim links to its source. Things you keep go to a personal **history board** (search, optional notes, remove, JSON export/import), stored on this device only. Issues are curated by hand in `history-issues.js`, separate from the renderer in `history.js`. Three issues so far: rights and Mary Wollstonecraft; the Luddites, Byron and Brontë's *Shirley*; and Olaudah Equiano and the debate over his birthplace. The paper uses Courier Prime (SIL Open Font License, bundled in `assets/fonts`, so it works offline).
- **data garden** (`#garden`): learn R by answering real research questions with open psychology data. R runs in the browser through [webR](https://docs.r-wasm.org/webr/) (downloaded from its CDN the first time a lesson opens, so that part needs a connection). Each question is a seed that blooms when you finish it and write a short field note; notes collect in a downloadable field journal, and each lesson's code downloads as a `.R` file for RStudio. Unit order follows MIT OpenCourseWare (18.05, 9.07, 9.63, 15.071, 18.650, 14.310x); lessons are original and link to the matching OCW material. Units 0–9 are written (29 lessons: R basics, description, probability and Bayes, estimation and the bootstrap, tests and power, experimental design, regression and ANOVA, logistic regression, reliability and PCA, and causal inference); datasets and their licences are listed in `data/README.md`.
- **tumble**, an original tunnel runner (`#tumble`): a little sprout with a tiny smile runs down a tunnel among the stars, jumping gaps; walk onto a wall and the tunnel turns so that wall becomes the floor. Sixty levels that ramp up gently (from four-sided tunnels up to twelve-sided ones, and faster as you go), each the same every time, with progress saved on this device. Arrow keys or a/d to move, space or ↑ to jump (hold longer to jump farther), or the on-screen buttons on touch screens.
- **just write**, an original local *forward-only writing space* built into quiet time. It deliberately follows the core idea of free-writing without editing, but **it is not the third-party justwrite.page website and does not sync with that service**. It automatically saves the current draft on this device. You can copy or download a .txt file. **new page** archives the existing page locally; archived pages can be downloaded from **previous pages**. Use downloads as backups because clearing browser storage deletes local drafts and archives.
- outside websites open their real sites in a separate browser tab/window, where allowed: murdoku, nyt games, murdle, and the little daily. Each also has a shortcut route (`#murdoku`, `#nyt`, `#murdle`, `#little-daily`) with an open button. During a quiet session these links go through the same eight-second early-exit pause.
- **mary oliver time**, **stretching time**, and **coloring time**: timer-only activities done away from the screen (`#mary-oliver`, `#stretching`, `#coloring`). Starting one starts a quiet session; it can be paused, survives a refresh, and ends with a quiet "time's up" (no sound or notification). The **light** menu sets an ambient glow (warm amber, rose, sage, lavender; still, breathing, or drifting waves; off to high) and can hide the countdown, which you can peek at by tapping. Reduced-motion systems default to still light.
- quiet sessions support sudoku, minesweeper, solitaire, tumble, data garden, the history paper, the psyc paper, just write, and the three timer activities
- offline app caching (once loaded), PWA installation and hash routes for shortcuts such as `/#write`

## github / vercel
Upload the **contents** of this folder to the **same github repository** used by your Vercel project: `index.html`, `style.css`, `app.js`, `minesweeper.js`, `sw.js`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `assets/`, and this README. Commit the changes; Vercel should redeploy automatically. Preset: Other. No build command or environment variables. The output directory is the repository root (`.`).

If an installed macOS/iPad app still looks like the old version, reload/reopen after deployment. The service-worker cache version was increased so the browser refreshes its offline copy.

## limitations
- All data stays in the local browser profile on the device (no syncing between Mac and iPad). Do not clear website data without downloading writing first.
- Just Write integration is a locally built alternative, **not an iframe of the original service**, whose framing policy and cross-site saved data are not guaranteed.
- Quiet sessions limit navigation only *within* the app. macOS apps and notifications require additional operating-system restrictions.

## apple shortcuts
In Apple's Shortcuts app, create a shortcut named **open quiet time**. Add the **URL** action containing `https://YOUR-APP.vercel.app/#home`, followed by **Open URLs**. On Mac you can assign a keyboard shortcut and enable **Use as Quick Action** in its Details. On iPad you can add the shortcut to the Home Screen or a widget. Duplicate and change the URL to `/#sudoku`, `/#minesweeper`, `/#solitaire`, `/#write`, etc. for quick-launch activities.

Shortcut URL launches may open the default browser rather than the standalone installed web-app window, depending on OS and link handling.

## stronger attention protection on macOS
1. Create a **Quiet Time Focus** in System Settings > Focus. Silence nearly all notifications; allow only people/apps you genuinely need. Use **Share Across Devices** if you want your iPad/iPhone to quiet down too.
2. **Do not rely only on app-triggered Focus:** on macOS, app-based Focus schedules turn off if you switch to another app. Instead, use a duration-based Focus, or an Apple Shortcut that activates the Focus *for a set duration* before opening quiet time.
3. For real website/app restrictions, use macOS **Screen Time** (Apps & Websites restrictions) or a blocker such as **Cold Turkey Blocker** with a locked block. Allowlist your garden URL and any external activities you deliberately want.
4. An HTML PWA cannot disable Command-Tab or Notification Center. Even a full-screen window is easy to leave.
5. The outside websites launch outside the garden and are not part of in-app quiet sessions. If you allow those sites, you'll need an external blocker to restrict other sites.

## iPad attention protection
Set up **Settings > Accessibility > Guided Access**, choose a passcode, open the installed quiet time web app, and begin a Guided Access session. This restricts you to a single app and works well with the built-in activities; launching external sites may leave the app or be prevented by the session.

## minesweeper controls
- Click/tap to reveal. The first reveal and its neighboring squares contain no mines.
- Right-click on Mac to place or remove a flag. On iPad, turn on **flag mode** then tap squares; a long press can also flag.
- You win by revealing every safe square. Large boards scroll horizontally on small screens.
